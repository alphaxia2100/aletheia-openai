# Decision on default `unlimited` for consequential investigations

**Decision: no.** At the audited runtime candidate, a consequential medical, legal, or policy investigation should not be permitted to enter the default `unlimited` mode without a human-set, recorded resource envelope. The mode may remain useful for explicitly non-decisional exploration, but it should not autonomously produce a strong claim, recommendation, or action premise. The decisive problem is that its stop condition is agent-paced convergence—no newly surfaced distinct origins or claims—not an operator-owned limit on expenditure, elapsed time, source coverage, or authority to continue. A completed citation score is valuable evidence bookkeeping; it is not a safety authorization or a proof that an open-ended search was sufficiently complete. [S1:59–78; S2:191–207, 210–252; S5:95–101]

## What the implementation actually bounds

If `init` receives neither a named thoroughness tier nor an explicit budget, it selects `unlimited`. Despite the documentation’s infinity notation, the frozen implementation assigns a numeric budget of 1,000,000, a maximum depth of 99, at most six children per split, and 512 total nodes. Splitting also requires enough budget for at least two scrutiny units and stops at the configured depth or node cap. Those are runaway backstops and tree-shape controls, not a human judgment that this particular patient, legal question, or policy choice merits 512 nodes or that the remaining uncertainty is tolerable. They also do not say how the budget maps to money, wall-clock time, token use, source classes, or irreversible downstream decisions. [S1:68–78, 126–143; S2:199–207, 210–252, 272–289]

The boundary is materially different for a bounded run. The leaf engine returns no round cap for `unlimited` and `max`; for other tiers it allows only `floor(node budget / scrutiny unit)` rounds and exits rather than silently overrunning the selected tier. A human can therefore select `quick`, `standard`, `deep`, `exhaustive`, or an explicit custom budget, but an explicit budget alone is only a starting control: the high-stakes envelope must also state aggregate limits and the decision owner. In particular, it should predeclare maximum elapsed time, nodes, investigation rounds, retrieval passes, selected and successful reads, manual/linked-primary rereads, spend/tokens, allowed channels and data classes, and a cancellation/escalation authority. The present paths expose some of those quantities but do not establish a single run-wide enforcement point for all of them. [S2:201–206, 210–234, 517–527; S3:495–517; S4:229–287]

There are useful local safeguards, but they do not close that gap. Each triage round permits only an initial candidate manifest plus one tighter requery, and an agent-selected read list is capped at that round’s suggested read count. Yet the unlimited loop can keep opening new rounds and leaves until the agent judges saturation. The runtime separately counts notes that were read outside the engine, rather than reserving them against a global allowance. The forward test demonstrates why configured rounds are not an adequate proxy: a dynamic candidate used 97 persisted read artifacts against a baseline’s 41—2.37 times as many—despite equal configured scrutiny rounds. High-stakes operation must enforce parity at the artifact and external-call level, not infer it later from round telemetry. [S3:43–44, 521–533, 708–760, 770–823; S4:248–287; S7:48–65]

## Why a strong claim is not yet credible

The current read-success predicate is not a document-identity predicate. A source becomes `_read_ok` after a successful full-text read of at least 1,500 characters; the code persists the returned body, but does not compare expected versus observed DOI, title, document type, or semantic identity. The operational observations record two successful reads whose observed GRADE documents conflicted with the requested ACM candidate identifiers, plus an anti-bot page accepted as a successful read. This is a direct route by which a superficially complete investigation could rely on the wrong document. A later isolated identity-gate experiment is encouraging but explicitly remains unpromoted and untested across legal, standards, books, forums, publishers, and broader calibration. [S3:376–410, 578–657; S5:37–98, 156–174; S6/BRANCHES:121–126]

Claim verification is also necessary but insufficient in its present form. The runtime can require final verdicts, hashes of `brief.md`, claims, and verdicts, and a named fresh-context auditor; its headline citation accuracy remains null until row verification and the hash-bound claim-scope attestation are valid. That is an important release condition. But the score aggregates independence over cited sources at the run level, while claim and verdict rows lack a requirement that each material conclusion have independently corroborating origins. The local hashes can also be rewritten by a fully privileged actor. Therefore a high-stakes claim cannot be promoted merely because `citation_complete` is true: it needs claim-specific evidence spans, polarity and magnitude checks, adverse evidence, source-origin analysis, and an independent reviewer who validates the exact final conclusion. [S1:225–266; S4:141–226, 462–504; S5:136–148]

## Required release gates

1. **Human-envelope gate.** Require an accountable human to select and record a bounded tier or custom budget plus the aggregate limits listed above before the run begins. Add enforced, monotonic accounting for all retrievals, engine reads, direct/manual reads, time, and spend; terminate on any limit and preserve an explicit incomplete/abstain state. The existing saturation rationale and 512-node cap may be retained only as secondary safety controls. [S1:70–78, 177–185; S2:191–207; S7:62–65]

2. **Semantic-read gate.** Before a source can support a claim, verify expected against observed identity and content state, preserve failed attempts, reject blocked/shell/wrong-body results, and require a full reread when a load-bearing source was truncated. Calibrate false accepts, false rejections, and resolver recovery on frozen medical, legal, and policy fixtures—not only on the reported exact-defect set. [S1:183–188; S3:598–609; S5:163–174]

3. **Whole-answer, claim-level gate.** Extract every load-bearing assertion from the final text, bind it to exact primary evidence and a final semantic verdict, rerun coverage after any edit, and require independent origins and disconfirming evidence for each strong conclusion rather than only for the run total. Use a fresh verifier at minimum; for consequential decisions, add qualified human domain review and an explicit abstention path when the evidence cannot meet the threshold. [S1:225–254; S4:186–226, 462–500; S5:136–139]

4. **Frozen, cost-matched evaluation gate.** Demonstrate the complete mechanism on preregistered, held-out, adversarial cases spanning the three high-stakes domains, with blinded comparison, human-calibrated scoring, complete artifact accounting, and failure/abstention measurements. Do not promote from the current small set: the forward record itself calls two topics mechanism-level evidence rather than a general win rate, and identifies degraded channel coverage and the absence of a human-calibrated multi-topic benchmark. [S7:41–42, 52–65, 67–72]

5. **Runtime and deployment integrity gate.** Pin the executable closure and effective channel configuration; publish a clean, independently reproducible release with signed-tag/SBOM/CI provenance; and make network, browser, credential, and host isolation explicit operator controls. Branch records say experimental mechanisms stay isolated until a frozen promotion gate clears, while the portability candidate expressly disclaims OS-sandbox and security certification. These controls prevent a credible claim from being attached to an ambiguous or unsafe runtime, but they do not substitute for the four evidence gates. [S2:86–123, 246–252; S6/BRANCHES:3–8, 110–126; S6/PORTABILITY:3–7, 151–164, 230–235]

**Self-check.** I rechecked the decision against the code’s actual unlimited caps, round behavior, read predicate, scoring gates, observed failure record, and the stated promotion limitations. The conclusion remains calibrated: bounded controls and verification features are real improvements, but they do not justify un-enveloped default-unlimited operation or a strong high-stakes claim at this release point. [S2:199–207; S3:495–517, 598–609; S4:462–504; S5:83–101]
