# Creatine & the brain — does supplementation actually help cognition?

**Run:** aletheia-research 0.4 `max` · 6 framings → 6 leaves · 321 sources, 57 primaries read in full,
280 independent origins, echo 0.13 (low = genuine cross-origin agreement). All 6 load-bearing numbers
re-verified against the primaries by an independent second model (Sonnet) — all 6 confirmed, no
polarity/magnitude errors.

## Bottom line
**Conditional, not yes/no.** For a healthy, well-rested, young omnivore, creatine is **not** a reliable
cognitive enhancer — the settling regulatory review (EFSA 2024) found no established cause-and-effect,
and pooled effects in the young are ~zero. But the benefit is real and **moderate-to-large in the
conditions theory predicts**: older adults, and acute brain-energy **stress** (sleep deprivation,
hypoxia). And a large part of the "null" in healthy people is plausibly **under-dosing**, because the
brain takes up creatine far more slowly and sparingly than muscle.

## Verified numbers (second-model checked against primaries)
- **Regulatory verdict (decisive):** EFSA NDA Panel 2024 — *"a cause-and-effect relationship has not
  been established between creatine supplementation and an improvement in cognitive function."*
  [doi 10.2903/j.efsa.2024.9100 — verified verbatim]
- **Pooled memory effect is small, age-split:** Prokopidis 2023 — overall memory SMD **0.29** (95% CI
  0.04–0.53); older adults 66–76 SMD **0.88** (0.22–1.55, I²=83%, **only 2 trials**); young adults
  **0.03** (ns). Overall/global cognition and executive function pool to non-significant. [verified]
- **The most-positive meta-analysis was rejected:** EFSA faulted Xu 2024 for **double-counting**
  (pooling non-independent tests from the same studies → inflated n); within Xu, global cognition
  SMD 0.34 (CI −0.20–0.88), **p=0.22 (ns)**. [verified; Xu has a published corrigendum]
- **Stress-gated benefit:** a single 0.35 g/kg dose under sleep deprivation improved processing/memory
  **+10–29%** (Gordji-Nejad 2024, n=15) — **single-lab, unreplicated**; loaded dosing corroborated in
  separate labs under 24 h sleep-dep (McMorris 2006, 20 g/d×7 d) and hypoxia (Turner 2015). [verified]
- **Depression = mood, not cognition:** Lyoo 2012 augmentation **d=1.13** on HAM-D (n=52) — an
  antidepressant-adjunct effect with no cognitive testing; **no large placebo-controlled TBI RCTs
  exist**. [verified]

## The mechanism that reconciles the mixed results (the crux)
Brain creatine uptake is slow and capped: **~+8.7%** brain total creatine on 20 g/d over 4 weeks
(Dechent 1999) vs **~18–20%** in muscle within days. The blood-brain barrier has low creatine-transporter
(SLC6A8) density and the brain makes much of its own, so oral creatine is a *secondary* input. Moving
brain creatine needs **~≥20 g/d (or ≥4 g/d for months)** — well above the 3–5 g/d most cognition trials
used, and a dose-ranging MRS study shows 10 g roughly *doubles* the brain response vs 2 g. So many
"null" trials are plausibly under-dosed for the brain, and benefit surfaces exactly where phosphocreatine
buffering is most demanded (sleep-dep, hypoxia, aging). A 2024 twist: a single ~25 g dose raised brain
phosphocreatine within *hours* in sleep-deprived subjects — challenging the "weeks-only" dogma.

## Where experts disagree, and why
- **Prokopidis (pro-subgroup) vs EFSA/critics:** the "improves memory" headline is a *unit-of-analysis
  artifact* that survives correction only in older adults — flagged independently by EFSA, a 2023 letter,
  and a 2026 commentary. Defensible statement: "creatine may aid memory in older adults, on limited
  data," **not** "creatine improves memory."
- **McMorris (mechanism champion):** his 2024 review calls overall effects "equivocal" and titles the
  theoretical basis a *failure* for the general case — yet still singles out stressed populations. Both
  camps converge: benefit is **conditional** (stress / age / low-baseline), not general.

## Practical read
- Healthy, rested, young, omnivore → expect little-to-no cognitive benefit at 3–5 g/d. (Muscle/strength
  benefits are separate and well-established.)
- Sleep-deprived / altitude / older / vegetarian → the best case for a real effect; if the *brain* is
  the target, the literature implies higher/longer dosing (≥10 g/d, sustained) than the muscle dose.
- Safety: 3–5 g/d monohydrate is well-tolerated; brain-targeting higher doses are less studied long-term.

## Honest gaps
- No large (n>500), high-quality RCT of realistic chronic doses (3–5 g/d) on cognition exists; positive
  signals cluster in tiny, high-dose, short loading trials.
- Acute sleep-dep result is single-lab; hypoxia rests on one trial; vegetarian>omnivore rests
  essentially on one RCT (Benton & Donohoe 2011, where meat-eaters actually did *worse*).
- The "brain is underdosed" fix and the first (uncontrolled) 2025 AD trial are promising but the decisive
  experiment — a 5 vs 20 g/d brain-MRS + cognition RCT — has not been run.
